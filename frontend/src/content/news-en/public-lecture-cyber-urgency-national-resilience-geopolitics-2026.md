On Saturday, September 26, 2026, **STMIK Tazkia** held a **Public Lecture** themed **"The Urgency of Cybersecurity in National Resilience & Indonesia's Geopolitics: Building a Sovereign, Excellent, and Noble Digital Generation"**. The event took place at the **Bogor City DPRD Office**, Jl. Pemuda No.26, Tanah Sareal, Bogor, West Java, and was attended by all STMIK Tazkia students and academic community members.

![Group photo of all STMIK Tazkia Public Lecture attendees at the Bogor City DPRD Office](/images/news/kuliah-umum-urgensi-siber-stmik-tazkia-foto-bersama.jpg)

## Why Cybersecurity and Geopolitics?

In an increasingly complex global geopolitical landscape, cybersecurity is no longer merely a technical issue — it is a critical pillar of national resilience. Cyberattacks on critical infrastructure, strategic data theft, and digital information warfare pose real threats to national sovereignty. This public lecture was designed to provide students with a comprehensive understanding of the relationship between cybersecurity, geopolitics, and the role of the younger generation in safeguarding Indonesia's digital sovereignty.

## Four Speakers with Cross-Cutting Perspectives

The public lecture featured four speakers representing legislative, media, and cybersecurity perspectives:

### 1. H. Iwan Suryanawan, S.Sos — Vice Chairman of the West Java Provincial DPRD

H. Iwan Suryanawan opened the session with a legislative perspective on the role of public policy in strengthening national cyber defense. He emphasized that regional governments bear significant responsibility in promoting digital literacy and cybersecurity at the community level.

"Cyber resilience is not solely the responsibility of the central government. Every region must have the capacity to protect its digital infrastructure. Young people like STMIK Tazkia students are at the forefront of building cyber defense from the local to the national level," stated H. Iwan Suryanawan.

![H. Iwan Suryanawan delivering his speech at the Bogor City DPRD podium](/images/news/kuliah-umum-urgensi-siber-stmik-tazkia-iwan-suryanawan.jpg)

### 2. Dr. Adityawarman Adil, S.Si., M.Si — Chairman of the Bogor City DPRD

As the host welcoming the event at the Bogor City DPRD building, Dr. Adityawarman Adil shared his perspective on how digital transformation in government must be balanced with strengthened cybersecurity. He also highlighted the importance of synergy between higher education institutions and the government in building a secure digital ecosystem.

"Bogor is transforming into a smart city. But a smart city without strong cyber defense is like a house without a lock. We greatly appreciate STMIK Tazkia for educating its students about the urgency of cybersecurity in a geopolitical context. This is an investment in the future of the nation," said Dr. Adityawarman Adil.

![Dr. Adityawarman Adil delivering his welcome remarks as Chairman of Bogor City DPRD](/images/news/kuliah-umum-urgensi-siber-stmik-tazkia-adityawarman-adil.jpg)

### 3. Ricky Setiadi, S.Kom., M.Kom — Indonesian Cyber Security Expert, STMIK Tazkia Lecturer

[Ricky Setiadi](/en/lecturers/ricky-setiadi) delivered the core material on the global cyber threat landscape and Indonesia's position within it. With extensive experience in [cybersecurity](/en/programs/computer-engineering) and numerous international certifications, he explained how cyberattacks have become geopolitical instruments used by nations to compete for influence in the digital era.

"Indonesia is a prime target for cyberattacks due to its large digital population and security infrastructure that still needs strengthening. Every [Computer Engineering](/en/programs/computer-engineering) and [Information Systems](/en/programs/information-systems) student must understand that their expertise is not just for personal careers — it is for safeguarding the nation's digital sovereignty. At STMIK Tazkia, we train students through [project-based learning](/en/about) that directly engages with real-world cybersecurity scenarios," explained Ricky Setiadi.

He also presented concrete examples of cyberattacks on critical infrastructure in various countries and how Indonesia must prepare to face similar threats.

### 4. Nihrawati, AS — Director of Radar Bogor

Nihrawati, AS brought a media perspective on information warfare and digital sovereignty. As Director of Radar Bogor, she discussed how media serves as both the front line and a target in modern cyber warfare, particularly through the spread of disinformation and public opinion manipulation.

"Cyber warfare is not always about hacking computer systems. Information warfare through digital media is the most dangerous form of cyberattack because it targets minds and national unity. The younger generation must be able to distinguish truthful information from manipulated content — this is the most fundamental form of cyber defense," said Nihrawati.

## Key Points Discussed

During the public lecture, several strategic issues were discussed in depth by the speakers:

### Digital Sovereignty as a Pillar of National Resilience

- **Critical infrastructure** — banking, energy, telecommunications, and transportation systems that are increasingly digitally connected have become prime targets for cyberattacks
- **Data as a strategic asset** — protecting citizens' data is a form of defending sovereignty in the digital era
- **Cross-border cyber warfare** — cyberattacks have become geopolitical instruments used in inter-state conflicts

### The Role of the Younger Generation in Cyber Defense

- **National cyber talent** — Indonesia needs more cybersecurity experts to secure its digital infrastructure
- **Ethical hacking** — technical skills must be balanced with ethics and noble character to ensure they are used to protect, not to harm
- **Digital literacy** — every digital citizen must understand cybersecurity fundamentals to protect themselves and their communities

### Synergy Between Government, Academia, and Media

- **Public policy** — cybersecurity regulations must continue to be strengthened at national and regional levels
- **Higher education** — universities play a role in producing competent and integrity-driven cyber talent
- **Media** — plays a role in public education and combating disinformation that threatens national unity
