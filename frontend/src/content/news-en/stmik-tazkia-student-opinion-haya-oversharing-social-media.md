**Muhammad Andreana Yusuf**, a student in the Informatics Engineering program at **STMIK Tazkia**, wrote an opinion piece published by the online outlet **Liputan9** on **November 15, 2026**. The article, titled **"Haya' di Era Oversharing: Mengapa Tidak Semua Hal Layak Dibagikan ke Ruang Publik"** (Haya' in the Age of Oversharing: Why Not Everything Belongs in the Public Space), looks at *oversharing*, the habit of posting too much personal information on social media, from the perspective of Islamic values.

![Illustration: protecting privacy amid social media's culture of sharing](/images/news/mahasiswa-stmik-tazkia-opini-haya-era-oversharing.jpeg)

## When the Line Between Private and Public Blurs

Andreana observes that social media has thinned the line between private life and the public space. Many people post their achievements, family problems and workplace conflicts, often to seek sympathy or simply to collect likes.

He argues that Islam has always paid close attention to protecting privacy. The key value is **haya'**, a sense of modesty or moral sensitivity that keeps a person from exposing what should stay private. He cites a hadith narrated by Ibn Majah (no. 4181) stating that every religion has its own character, and the character of Islam is modesty.

## Three Reasons to Protect Privacy

Andreana outlines three things that are safeguarded when haya' guides how we use social media:

- **Covering one's own faults.** He warns against openly displaying one's own wrongdoing, citing hadiths narrated by Bukhari (no. 6069) and Muslim (no. 2990).
- **Protecting peace of mind.** Showing off (*flexing*) can breed envy and other spiritual ailments, including the danger of *'ain*.
- **Respecting the privacy and dignity of others.** He criticizes sharing screenshots of private conversations to discredit people, citing Surah Al-Hujurat verse 12 on fault-finding and backbiting.

## Ask Before You Post

In his conclusion, Andreana stresses that social media is only a tool. It can spread knowledge and goodness, but it can also leave a harmful digital footprint. Before posting, he suggests asking not "will this go viral?" but "do others need to know this?" and "does it bring benefit in this world and the next?".
